# Overview of BHF Data Science Centre 

BHF Data Science Centre presents itself as a national research infrastructure and service provider supporting researchers to discover, access and use health data and reusable research assets for cardiovascular research. Its services cover the research lifecycle from data access and linkage through data preparation, analysis-ready resources, public involvement and delivery of research outputs. [Research services page](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/research-services-new/) 

Its engagement model is described as collaborative and tailored to individual projects. As a non-profit organisation, it delivers services on a cost-recovery basis and also provides self-service materials including curated data products, tools, best-practice guidance and training resources. [Research services page](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/research-services-new/) 

The retrieved pages group its offering into three broad areas: 

Accessing and linking cardiovascular health data. 

Preparing and using health data. 

Involving patients and the public in cardiovascular data research. 

[Research services page](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/research-services-new/) 

# Research and Data Services offered 

## Data access, discovery and linkage 

BHF Data Science Centre brings cardiovascular health data together and makes it securely accessible. Its stated purpose is to help data owners and researchers identify insights that benefit patients and the public. [Data access and linkage](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/data-access-and-linkage/) 

The retrieved services include: 

Population data: support for secure, streamlined access to linked whole-population health data, including NHS national datasets, audits and registries. Population data 

Consented cohort data: linkage of cohort data to routinely collected NHS data through the UK Clinical Cohorts Trusted Research Environment, or UK CliC. UK CliC TRE 

Data discovery and project feasibility: tools and support to help researchers establish what data are available and whether the data are suitable for their research questions. UK CliC TRE 

Linkage coordination: management of the data-linkage application process for cohort studies seeking linkage to NHS data from across the UK. UK CliC TRE 

UK CliC researcher support: feasibility assessment, data engineering, curation and PPIE support while researchers develop proposals to use cohort data. UK CliC for researchers 

Cohort onboarding: review of consent and governance materials, project scoping, contracting, secure data transfer, linkage and access to linked data through the SAIL Databank Information Governance Review Panel process. UK CliC cohort studies 

Local and regional NHS data and imaging-data services are labelled “Coming soon” on the data-access page. Data access and linkage 

## Data curation and engineering 

The Centre provides data curation and engineering intended to transform complex health data into research-ready datasets more efficiently, consistently and reproducibly. Its team combines technical expertise, reusable pipelines and experience across UK Trusted Research Environments. [Data curation and engineering services](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/data-curation-and-engineering-services/) 

Retrieved service components include: 

Assessing project feasibility, expected data coverage and completeness. 

Implementing computable phenotypes and codelists. 

Creating cohorts, exposures, outcomes and covariates. 

Developing documented, version-controlled and repeatable curation pipelines. 

Adapting pipelines to differences in datasets and governance across national, regional, cohort and disease-specific research environments. 

Supporting linked studies spanning England, Scotland and Wales. 

Data curation and engineering services 

The page says these reusable approaches have helped research teams reduce project set-up time, improve completeness and consistency through linkage and harmonisation, standardise variable derivation, support reproducible analysis across TREs and accelerate research delivery. Data curation and engineering services 

## Curated assets 

The Centre describes curated assets as harmonised, documented and reusable tables, code components and data-preparation approaches. They are developed with research teams, clinicians, data custodians and programme partners in response to research-community needs. [Curated assets](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/curated-assets/) 

Examples retrieved were: 

A harmonised demographics table covering month and year of birth, sex, ethnicity, deprivation, geographical region and date of death. 

Standardised definitions and code for identifying people with evidence of COVID-19 infection. 

Reusable approaches for preparing Hospital Episode Statistics Admitted Patient Care diagnoses. 

Standardised preparation of HES APC procedure information. 

Harmonised mortality variables and derivation approaches. 

Curated assets 

Publicly shared supporting resources can include README documentation, methodologies, variable definitions, version-controlled code and guidance for adapting and reusing the assets. Curated assets 

## Computable phenotypes and codelists 

The Centre supports researchers to develop, use and share computable phenotypes through guidance, tools and pipelines. It also provides a library of openly available, quality-assured phenotype definitions. [Computable phenotypes and codelists](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/computable-phenotypes-and-codelists/) 

The retrieved services and resources include: 

Phenotype definitions shared through the HDR UK Phenotype Library and linked GitHub repositories. 

Community-agreed, validated phenotypes containing implementation code, codelists, detailed README files and algorithm flowcharts. 

Curated phenotypes for diabetes, chronic kidney disease and myocardial infarction. 

Lookup tables, terminology-mapping tables and additional packages for code exploration and codelist development. 

Bespoke phenotype and codelist development, review, implementation, validation and methodological advice. 

Guidance and tools for FAIR sharing of phenotype definitions. 

Computable phenotypes and codelists

# Data assets and platforms 

## UK Clinical Cohorts Trusted Research Environment 

UK CliC is a secure platform through which consented clinical cohort data can be stored, linked to routinely collected NHS data, analysed and safely shared for public-benefit health research. It supports both cohort studies wishing to link and share data and researchers applying to use approved cohort datasets. [UK CliC TRE](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/uk-clic-trusted-research-environment/) 

UK CliC is hosted in the Secure eResearch Platform at Swansea University. Linked anonymised datasets are made available to approved researchers through SAIL Databank. UK CliC TRE 

Supported cohort-data types listed on the site include clinical, biomarker and laboratory, demographic, imaging, pathology, personal monitoring, radiological, omics and molecular data. UK CliC cohort studies 

The page says researchers access cohort data through the SAIL Databank approval process, must complete safe-researcher training and cannot download data directly from the TRE. Outputs undergo disclosure-control checks before release. UK CliC for researchers

# Active projects and research themes 

## CVD-COVID-UK / COVID-IMPACT 

CVD-COVID-UK/COVID-IMPACT is described as a major research programme coordinated by BHF Data Science Centre, involving over 400 researchers from more than 50 organisations. It has enabled analysis of anonymised, linked, nationally collated UK healthcare data. [CVD-COVID-UK/COVID-IMPACT](https://bhfdatasciencecentre.org/research/data-access-and-linkage/population-data/cvd-covid-uk-covid-impact/) 

Its retrieved research themes are: 

How pre-existing cardiovascular and other conditions, risk factors and medications affect susceptibility to and adverse outcomes from COVID-19. 

The direct effects of COVID-19 on cardiovascular and other complications and medium- or longer-term health risks. 

The indirect effects of the pandemic, government response and NHS response on the presentation, diagnosis, management and outcomes of cardiovascular and other conditions. 

CVD-COVID-UK/COVID-IMPACT 

The retrieved page labels these projects as active: 

CCU001: effects of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and angiotensin-receptor blockers on COVID-19 outcomes, led by Jonathan Sterne at University of Bristol. 

CCU002: SARS-CoV-2 infection, vaccination and risk of vascular events, led by William Whiteley at University of Edinburgh. 

CCU003: direct and indirect effects of the COVID-19 pandemic in people with cardiovascular disease, led by Amitava Banerjee at University College London. 

CVD-COVID-UK/COVID-IMPACT 

The programme lead is identified as Angela Wood. The page also states that the programme’s data-sharing agreement for access to the NHS England Secure Data Environment ends in January 2027 and that no new projects are being accepted. CVD-COVID-UK/COVID-IMPACT

Its focus is using whole-population data to improve prevention, early detection, diagnosis and management of cancer, cardiovascular disease and other complex diseases. # Key capabilities and tools 

Population-scale linked-data research: access to and analysis of linked national health datasets in secure environments. 

Cohort linkage infrastructure: secure cohort storage, linkage to routine NHS data, controlled analysis and approved data reuse. 

Data discovery: dashboards supporting dataset identification, feasibility assessment and cross-nation comparison. 

Data curation and engineering: cleaning, harmonisation, cohort and variable construction, quality assurance and reusable version-controlled pipelines. 

Reusable research assets: standardised tables, cohorts, diagnosis and procedure transformations, mortality variables and supporting code. 

Phenotyping: validated phenotype definitions, terminology resources, lookup and mapping tables, implementation pipelines and sharing guidance. 

Codelist Comparison Tool: maps medical codes to descriptions, compares codes with the GDPPR reference set, associates codes with counts in the NHS England SDE, supports codelist construction and export, and accepts codelists from external libraries or users. Computable phenotypes and codelists 

Open and reproducible research: use of GitHub, the HDR UK Phenotype Library, documented processing decisions, reusable code and FAIR-sharing guidance. 

PPIE: public review, project design, workshops, community building, communications and accessible-material development.
